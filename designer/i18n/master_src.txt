IEC 60364-4-41 expects 30 mA RCD protection on socket-outlets and on circuits for portable equipment.
BS 7671 411.3.3 requires 30 mA RCD protection for socket-outlets rated up to 20 A and most final circuits in domestic premises.
NEC 210.8 and 210.12 call for GFCI and AFCI protection on many receptacle and branch circuits. Confirm the edition adopted locally.
AS/NZS 3000 requires RCD protection on final subcircuits for socket-outlets and lighting.
Marine AC installations normally use double-pole switching and 30 mA RCD protection on final circuits. Confirm against class and flag rules.
ABYC E-11 expects GFCI protection on receptacles and double-pole branch protection. Confirm against the current edition.
Extra-low-voltage AC: no RCD is needed for SELV circuits. Voltage drop is the usual limit on cable length.
Extra-low-voltage DC: no RCD is needed for SELV circuits. Size each breaker from the current (I = P ÷ V), protect the cable, and use DC-rated devices. Marine practice (ISO 10133, ABYC E-11) allows 3 % voltage drop for critical circuits such as navigation lights and bilge pumps and 10 % for the rest.
Checks cover design current against breaker and cable rating, voltage drop, RCD expectation, fault level, Lutron input, zone and temperature limits. Earth-fault loop impedance, discrimination and selectivity are not calculated here.
DBoard Designer is a design aid. Cable ratings, voltage-drop limits and Lutron data are indicative; check them against the current edition of your standard, the manufacturer's submittal and a qualified engineer before building.
